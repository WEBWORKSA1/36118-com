How to value a domain name without fooling yourself

Domains. 7-minute read. Updated 2026-10-10.

Every domain has two prices. The wholesale price is what another investor will pay quickly. The end-user price is what a business that needs the name will pay, which can be ten or more times higher but may take years to find. Most bad valuations come from mixing up the two.

Step 1: place the name in its class

For numerics, length and composition do most of the work: 3N above 4N above 5N, chips above names with 4, no leading zero, bonus for patterns. For word domains, the questions are length, extension, how common the words are, and whether businesses already spend money on them in advertising.

Step 2: find comparable sales

Look for public sales of names in the same class and extension within the last two or three years. One sale is an anecdote; five are a range. Prefer reported sales from marketplaces and industry sales charts over asking prices, which say nothing about what buyers actually paid.

Step 3: adjust for liquidity

How many buyers could plausibly want this name this year? A 3N.com has a deep pool of investor buyers. A two-word .net may have five potential end users in the world. The thinner the pool, the bigger the discount you should apply to any comparable.

Step 4: check for legal and technical risk

  • Does the name match a registered trademark? If so, its resale value may be close to nothing, and holding it can create legal exposure.
  • Is the name on spam blocklists from previous use?
  • Is the registration history clean, with no disputes?

Why automated appraisals mislead

Automated tools are useful for ranking names, but they often anchor to search volume and ignore liquidity. That is why our domain scorecard gives a liquidity score and shows every factor rather than producing a dollar figure. For a number you can act on, combine the score with real comparables, or request a human appraisal.

A useful test: if you would not accept your own estimate as an offer today, it is an end-user dream, not a valuation.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between wholesale and retail domain value?

Wholesale is what investors pay quickly; retail or end-user value is what a business that needs the name pays, usually after a longer search.

Are automated domain appraisals accurate?

They are fine for ranking but unreliable as prices. Use comparable sales for anything you plan to act on.

How long does it take to sell a premium domain?

It varies widely. Liquid classes like short numerics can sell within weeks at wholesale; end-user sales can take years.
